# Art Direction

## Baseline

Ashenblade Arena is **bright, vivid high fantasy**.

The world should feel adventurous, colorful, magical, readable, and immediately appealing. Darkness is allowed—even important—but it should create contrast instead of defining every location.

## Generative art prompt rules

When generating environment, creature, equipment, spell, resource, landmark, or UI art, default to:

- Rich, saturated colors with intentional palette separation.
- Clear readable lighting and strong focal points.
- Strong silhouettes that remain identifiable at game-scale presentation.
- Luminous magical accents, enchanted materials, colorful particles, and expressive atmospheric effects where appropriate.
- Detailed fantasy materials: polished steel, glowing crystal, carved wood, colored cloth, vibrant flora, ancient stone, enchanted glass, unusual metals, and magical energy.
- A sense of wonder and adventure before grimness.
- Visually distinct biomes with gradual geographic transitions.

## Darkness and horror

Cursed forests, haunted ruins, necromantic regions, volcanic wastes, plague zones, deep caves, tragic storylines, grotesque monsters, and unsettling magic are all valid.

Those areas should still use deliberate visual composition and readable color. A dark location can contain bright spell light, glowing fungi, strange crystals, lava, moonlight, spectral color, banners, blood-red foliage, or other visual anchors rather than collapsing into muddy gray-brown imagery.

## Avoid as a default

- Uniform grimdark treatment across the entire game.
- Muddy low-contrast palettes.
- Every settlement looking ruined or impoverished.
- Every forest being haunted.
- Excessive gray/brown desaturation.
- Generic fantasy assets without a memorable silhouette or material hook.

## Cinderwatch

The first hub should communicate safety, possibility, and frontier adventure: warm roofs and banners, lively market color, bright forge light, visible greenery, readable stonework, and active construction sites. The ancient black-stone beacon can provide a darker visual anchor without making the settlement itself oppressive.
